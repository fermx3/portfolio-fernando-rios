## Project Overview

**Nebluna** is the operations system for a specialty coffee micro-roastery in Mexico City. Green coffee arrives in lots, gets roasted into batches, packaged into bags, sold across four channels and shipped. The app covers that whole cycle, and it is the only place the business now records any of it.

The interesting part was never the CRUD. The roastery already ran on a spreadsheet, which is where nearly every small business starts and which works fine while the catalogue still fits in your head. The project is mostly the story of replacing it without losing what it already knew about the trade.

## The Problem

To a spreadsheet, `BOLSA-250` and `BOLSA-250-VALV` look too much alike. If the formula that totals them compares text, both supplies come off the same balance and the gap does not surface until the next physical count. That is not a mistake by whoever wrote the formula: a sheet has no way to declare that they are two different things.

The pattern repeats across the whole domain. Stock should not be able to go negative. A sale should not be able to exist without pointing at a specific lot. A blend should not be deducted twice. These are rules a cell can remember but cannot enforce.

So the migration started with an audit rather than a schema. Much of the logic lived in Google-only functions (`QUERY`, `FILTER`, `LET`, `SCAN`) with no SQL equivalent, so it was not translated: it was rebuilt as views and application logic. That is slower than porting it, and the only way the result stays explainable.

That is where the schema's hardest rule comes from: relate rows by foreign key, never by matching text. The original error stops being expressible.

## Architecture

### Data Layer
**PostgreSQL** on **Supabase**, with 99 versioned migrations from `001_create_base_tables.sql` onward. The invariants live here, not in the forms: stock cannot go negative because a trigger refuses the write, a sale must reference a concrete packaged-inventory row, and a blend deducts from its source lots through `blend_components` and nowhere else.

That last one is a scar. Deducting a blend both through its components and through a manual adjustment double-counted the loss, so the canonical path is now singular and documented. The repository keeps a numbered register of that kind of finding, and reading it is a precondition for touching the schema.

### Application Layer
**Next.js 16** with the App Router, organised into three route groups: authentication, the dashboard, and a print group that renders a letter-sized sale ticket with its own stylesheet. Server state is **React Query**, forms are **react-hook-form** validated with **Zod**, and the interface is **shadcn/ui** over **Tailwind CSS 4**.

It is built mobile first, because a good share of the sales get recorded standing up, at a market stall or in a customer's shop, not at a desk. The dense screens, reports and reconciliation, are the ones that assume a laptop.

### Roles & Access
Two roles, `owner` and `sales_rep`, which in practice are two different products: administration sees financials, costs and settings; each sales rep sees the catalogue, their own sales and their own commissions, and nothing belonging to anyone else. The separation is enforced by **Row Level Security** policies on every table, so it holds even if a route guard is wrong. The interface hides what a rep should not see; the database is what makes sure it cannot be fetched.

## Domain Rules

The rules are the product, and most of them are unintuitive until someone explains the trade:

- **Shrinkage is measured, not estimated.** Roasting loses 12 to 20% of the green weight to moisture. Green in minus roasted out is recorded per batch, which is the single biggest lever on true cost per kilo.
- **Lot traceability is mandatory.** Every sale points at the packaged lot it came from, so "which roast was this bag?" is answerable when a customer reports a defect.
- **Roasted coffee is IVA exempt** under article 2-A of the Mexican VAT law, so any calculation that multiplies or divides by 1.16 is broken by definition.
- **Commission is rate times subtotal, and only on the Promotor channel.** Direct, WhatsApp and Instagram sales earn none.
- **Business dates are `DATE`, never `TIMESTAMPTZ`.** Storing one as a timestamp shifted every app-captured date back a day before anyone noticed.
- **Lot codes are designed to be handwritten**, on a label and read aloud during a count.

## Testing & CI

Eighteen test files: 9 unit suites covering 86 cases with no database, and 9 integration suites that exercise the parts where correctness actually lives: RLS policies, the sale lifecycle, commissions, repackaging, inventory reconciliation, date handling and stock by SKU.

Four GitHub Actions workflows guard the branch. Lint is blocking at zero errors. The integration workflow spins up a complete Supabase stack inside the runner, applies all 99 migrations from scratch and runs the suites against it, so it never touches production and never depends on its state. A Lighthouse workflow audits the stable domain on every production deploy, with accessibility and best practices gated at 0.95; SEO is switched off on purpose, because an internal app behind a login has no business being indexed. A fourth workflow compares the migration files against the remote registry and fails on drift.
